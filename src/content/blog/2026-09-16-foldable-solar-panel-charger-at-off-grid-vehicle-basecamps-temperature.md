## Temperature Ratings and Their Impact

### Cold Weather Performance
In colder climates, the efficiency of solar panels decreases significantly due to reduced solar radiation intensity and lower ambient temperatures. For instance, at -10°C (14°F), a typical crystalline silicon panel might operate only around 65% of its rated capacity compared to optimal conditions (25°C or 77°F). This drop in efficiency can severely impact the ability to charge devices during winter expeditions.

### Hot Weather Considerations
Conversely, high temperatures can also degrade performance. At temperatures above 30°C (86°F), solar panels may experience thermal stress, leading to reduced output and potentially shortened lifespan. For example, at 50°C (122°F), a panel's efficiency might drop by as much as 15-20%.

### Real-World Examples
Consider a situation where you are setting up an off-grid basecamp in the Himalayas during winter. The average temperature is -8°C (17°F) with occasional dips to -20°C (-4°F). A foldable solar panel rated for optimal performance at 25°C would struggle, possibly providing only half of its expected charge output.
