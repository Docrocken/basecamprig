# Ultralight Wood Burning Titanium Stove at Remote Winter Basecamp Setups: Essential Accessories and Modular Rig Configuration

## Introduction to the Ultralight Wood Burning Titanium Stove
Ultralight wood burning titanium stoves have become a staple in remote winter basecamp setups due to their compact size, durability, and efficiency. These stoves are designed for minimalist backpackers who require a reliable source of heat and water boiling without sacrificing weight or space. In this guide, we will explore the essential accessories and modular rig configuration needed to set up an ultralight wood burning titanium stove at remote winter basecamps.

## Essential Accessories

### 1. Titanium Fire Pan
A high-quality titanium fire pan is crucial for containing your fire and preventing sparks from jumping. A 5-inch diameter titanium fire pan weighs approximately 30 grams, making it lightweight yet durable enough to withstand the rigors of winter conditions.

### 2. Heat Shield
A heat shield not only protects your stove but also reflects radiant heat back onto the baseplate, improving efficiency. A typical heat shield for an ultralight wood burning titanium stove measures about 6 inches in diameter and weighs around 35 grams.

### 3. Tinder and Fire Starter
For quick ignitions, you need reliable tinder and fire starters. Options include dry leaves, cotton balls soaked in Vaseline, or specialized fire starter sticks that weigh less than a gram each. A small pack of 20 fire starters will add only about 15 grams to your load.

### 4. Wood Collection Tools
Wood collection is essential but can be challenging in winter conditions. Lightweight tools like a folding saw (80 grams) and a hatchet (300-350 grams) are indispensable for gathering fuel. A small, collapsible axe with a built-in knife (250 grams) provides versatility.

### 5. Windscreen
A windscreen is critical in cold, windy conditions to ensure your fire remains stable and hot. A simple, lightweight windscreen can be made from aluminum foil or purchased as a folded, compact unit that expands into a robust shield. An effective windscreen for an ultralight stove weighs around 70 grams.

### 6. Driptrap
A driptrap prevents water from dripping onto the stove and causing a flare-up. A lightweight metal driptrap (15-20 grams) can be used effectively, but a more advanced silicone driptrap weighing only about 10 grams is highly recommended for its durability and ease of use.

### 7. Fire Extinguisher
Safety should always come first. A portable fire extinguisher, such as a small chemical fire extinguisher (50 grams), can be carried in your backpack to quickly address any flare-ups or unintended fires.

## Modular Rig Configuration

### Basecamp Setup
When setting up your basecamp, consider the layout and placement of your stove within the campsite. Use a tarp or heavy-duty ground cloth as a platform for your stove setup to protect it from wet conditions. Place the fire pan on this platform, ensuring it is secure and stable.

### Stove Placement
Position the titanium stove close to the center of your camp but away from tents or other flammable materials. The heat shield should be positioned around the stove to maximize efficiency and minimize direct exposure to the elements.

### Fuel Management
Store wood in a dry place near your stove setup, ideally under a tarp or sheltered area. This reduces moisture absorption and ensures you can easily access fuel when needed. Use a small, portable saw to cut wood into manageable pieces before storing them.

### Safety Protocols
Always have a fire extinguisher on hand and know how to use it in case of emergencies. Establish clear guidelines for campfire behavior among team members or group members to prevent accidental fires.
