## Field Maintenance: Drying Techniques

### Immediate Post-Run Actions
1. **Initial Removal of Moisture**: Upon returning to your basecamp or shelter, immediately remove excess snow from the exterior using a soft brush or towel.
2. **Air Circulation**: Place the shoes in a cool area with good air circulation, but avoid direct heat sources which can cause condensation.

### Drying Methods
1. **Natural Air Drying**:
   - **Placement**: Hang the shoes inside your tent or shelter, ensuring they are not touching any surfaces.
   - **Ventilation**: Use small gaps in your gear to allow air flow around the shoes.
2. **Using a Tent Stove**:
   - **Safety Measures**: Ensure proper ventilation and use of a fire extinguisher nearby.
   - **Placement**: Place the stove near but not directly on the shoes, using a metal mesh or heat shield.

### Advanced Techniques
1. **Heated Drying Racks**: Utilize portable electric racks with temperature control to maintain optimal drying conditions without overheating.
2. **Chemical Heating Paks**: Place these paks in the shoes to provide localized warmth and aid in evaporation, but ensure they are not too hot as to damage the membrane.

## Tear Repair Guide

### Identification of Tears
- **Visual Inspection**: Look for any visible tears or punctures on the exterior of the shoe.
- **Water Test**: Apply a few drops of water to suspect areas. If it beads and does not penetrate, the tear is minor; if it soaks in, immediate repair is necessary.

### Repair Tools and Materials
- **Adhesive Sealer**: Use GORE-TEX® PFL (Porous Film Liner) Repair Kit or similar high-quality waterproofing sealant.
- **Needles and Thread**: For larger tears, a needle and thread to sew the fabric back together are essential.

### Step-by-Step Repair Process
1. **Preparation**:
   - Clean the area thoroughly with a soft cloth to remove any dirt or debris.
   - Apply an even layer of sealer around the edges of the tear to ensure proper adhesion.
2. **Sealing Small Tears**:
   - Apply the sealer in a thin, continuous line over the tear.
   - Allow it to dry completely before walking or using the shoes again.
3. **Repairing Larger Tears**:
   - Cut a patch slightly larger than the tear from an extra section of the same fabric.
   - Sew the edges of the patch to the shoe using a needle and thread, ensuring they are tightly secured.
   - Apply sealer around the perimeter of the patch for added protection.

### Preventive Maintenance
- **Regular Inspection**: Check your shoes regularly during runs to catch any potential issues early.
- **Proper Storage**: Store shoes in a cool, dry place when not in use to prevent mold and mildew growth.
- **Use of Protective Gear**: Consider using trail socks or booties over your running shoes to protect them from sharp objects and excessive moisture.
