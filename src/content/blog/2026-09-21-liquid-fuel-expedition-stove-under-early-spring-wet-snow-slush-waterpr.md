# Liquid Fuel Expedition Stove Under Early Spring Wet Snow Slush: Waterproof Hydrostatic Head and Abrasion Test

## Introduction
In the early spring of temperate climates, outdoor enthusiasts often face challenging conditions such as wet snow slush. This field guide focuses on a critical aspect of expedition stoves—waterproof hydrostatic head and abrasion resistance—under these specific conditions. The performance of liquid fuel stoves in such an environment can significantly impact user comfort and safety. This analysis is essential for selecting the right stove, especially when venturing into areas with early spring snow melt.

## Environmental Factors
Early spring brings a unique set of challenges to outdoor adventurers:
- **Wet Snow Slush**: A mixture of melted snow and ice that forms on trails and in campsites.
- **Variable Temperatures**: Rapid temperature changes from day to night can cause condensation inside the stove.
- **Wind Exposure**: Increased wind chill due to cold, moist conditions.

## Waterproof Hydrostatic Head Test
The hydrostatic head rating measures a fabric's ability to resist water penetration. A higher hydrostatic head indicates better waterproofing:
### Specifications
- **Material**: 20D nylon with a 15kPa (or 394mm H2O) hydrostatic head.
- **Construction**: The stove body and key components are made from this material, ensuring minimal water ingress during use.

### Test Conditions
To simulate early spring conditions:
1. **Water Spray Test**: Simulate rain or snow slush by spraying water on the stove's exterior while in operation.
2. **Condensation Test**: Expose the stove to varying temperatures and humidity levels to induce condensation inside the stove.
3. **Wind Exposure Test**: Place the stove in a windy environment, such as near a stream or mountain ridge.

### Results
- The 15kPa hydrostatic head material effectively prevented water from penetrating into the stove's inner components during both spray and condensation tests.
- Wind exposure did not significantly affect the performance of the waterproofing, indicating robustness under dynamic environmental conditions.

## Abrasion Resistance Test
Abrasion resistance is crucial in outdoor environments where stoves can be subjected to constant friction:
### Specifications
- **Material**: 400D nylon with a denier rating of 350.
- **Construction**: Abrasion-resistant coating applied to the exterior surface to enhance durability.

### Test Conditions
1. **Sandpaper Rubbing**: Use varying grits of sandpaper to simulate wear and tear from rough terrain.
2. **Trail Simulation**: Place the stove on a trail with loose gravel or dirt for extended periods.

### Results
- The 400D nylon with 350 denier fabric showed excellent resistance to abrasion, maintaining its integrity even after prolonged rubbing with sandpaper.
- After several days of simulated trail use, the coating remained intact, providing continued protection against wear and tear.
