# Packable Camp Hatchet Across Rocky Scree and Steep Mountain Passes: Waterproof Hydrostatic Head and Abrasion Test

## Introduction
When venturing into the rugged terrain of rocky scree and steep mountain passes, a reliable packable camp hatchet is an essential tool. This guide delves deep into the technical specifications and performance tests required for such a hatchet to ensure it can withstand the harsh conditions faced by outdoor enthusiasts and basecamp operators.

## Key Specifications
### 1. Waterproofing: Hydrostatic Head Test
The ability of a hatchet to resist water penetration is crucial, especially when traveling through damp or wet environments. The hydrostatic head test measures the pressure in millimeters (mm) that the fabric can withstand before it begins to leak. A higher hydrostatic head indicates better waterproof performance.

- **Recommended Hydrostatic Head:** At least 3000 mm H2O
- **Explanation:** A hatchet with a 3000 mm H2O rating means its material can resist water pressure equivalent to about 3 meters of standing water. This ensures that any moisture will bead off the surface rather than penetrate, keeping your gear dry.

### 2. Abrasion Resistance: Denier and Fabric Type
Abrasion resistance is vital for a hatchet used in rocky terrains where it may come into frequent contact with rugged surfaces. The fabric’s denier rating reflects its strength and durability under wear and tear.

- **Recommended Denier:** 600D or higher
- **Explanation:** A 600D polyester or nylon fabric offers substantial abrasion resistance, making it suitable for outdoor use. Higher denier ratings provide better protection against cuts, scrapes, and tears from rough surfaces like scree.

### 3. Insulation and Breathability: R-value and Fill Power
While not typically a primary feature of hatchets, considering the insulation properties can be useful in extreme cold conditions where you might need to use it as part of your basecamp setup.

- **Recommended R-value:** N/A (Hatchets are generally used in warmer climates)
- **Explanation:** For environments where temperature regulation is crucial, look for an insulated handle with a high R-value. However, most hatchet handles are uninsulated and made from materials like metal or wood, which do not offer significant insulating properties.

### 4. Weight and Packability
The hatchet should be lightweight and packable to ensure it doesn’t add unnecessary bulk to your load. A compact design allows for easy storage in a backpack or gear bag.

- **Weight:** Approximately 1 kg (2.2 lbs)
- **Packability:** The hatchet should fit snugly into a dedicated sheath that can be rolled up and packed inside a small compartment of your [backpack]([https://www.amazon.com/s?k=](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=)backpack+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21).

## Technical Breakdown
### Design Considerations
The design of the hatchet must cater to its intended use in rugged terrain. Key features include:

- **Handle Material:** Typically made from high-strength aluminum or steel for durability.
- **Blade Shape and Size:** A compact, versatile blade that can handle a variety of tasks such as cutting branches, chopping wood, and carving stakes.

### Performance Testing
To ensure the hatchet meets the required standards, it undergoes rigorous testing protocols:

#### 1. Hydrostatic Head Test
This test simulates real-world conditions where the hatchet might be exposed to heavy rain or snow. The blade is immersed in a chamber filled with water, and pressure is gradually increased until moisture penetrates the material.

- **Test Conditions:** A controlled environment with precise temperature and humidity levels.
- **Results:** A pass/fail criterion based on whether any water passes through the fabric at 3000 mm H2O pressure. Any leak indicates failure.

#### 2. Abrasion Test
This test evaluates the hatchet's resistance to wear from rough surfaces such as scree or rocks.

- **Test Conditions:** The blade is repeatedly abraded against a hard surface under controlled conditions.
- **Results:** A pass/fail criterion based on how many cycles the hatchet can withstand before showing signs of damage. Typically, it should maintain its integrity after 1000 cycles without failing.

### Field Testing
Field testing involves taking the hatchet into the real-world environment to ensure all performance characteristics hold up under actual use conditions:

- **Scree Test:** The hatchet is tested on rocky scree to simulate conditions commonly found in mountain passes.
- **Passage Test:** Simulating steep mountain passes by using the hatchet to clear paths and create stable steps.
