### Engineering Specs

- **Hydrostatic Head**: Merino wool has a hydrostatic head of approximately 20 mm. This means it can resist water penetration up to 20 mm of static water column height, which is adequate for most packrafting conditions.
- **Fabric Denier**: The denier range for merino wool socks suitable for fast-and-light packrafting is typically between 40 and 65, ensuring durability without compromising breathability.

### Construction Techniques

### Layering System

In colder conditions, layering is essential. A base layer of synthetic or wool socks can be worn underneath the merino wool hiking sock for additional warmth. This layered approach helps maintain a consistent temperature without over-heating in warm conditions.

### Maintenance and Care

- **Washing**: Hand washing with mild soap and air drying are recommended to preserve the integrity of the fibers.
- **Drying Time**: Merino wool dries relatively quickly, but ensure it is thoroughly dry before storing to prevent mold growth.
