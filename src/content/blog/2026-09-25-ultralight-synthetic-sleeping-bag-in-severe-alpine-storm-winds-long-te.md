## Field Analysis

### Testing Conditions

The sleeping bag was tested over multiple seasons, including spring snowstorms, summer thunderstorms, autumn cold fronts, and winter blizzards. These tests were conducted in areas such as the Swiss Alps, French Pyrenees, and Canadian Rockies, where alpine storm winds are a common occurrence.

### Wind Resistance

Alpine storms often feature high-speed winds that can strip heat from the body rapidly. The synthetic insulation's ability to retain warmth despite wind chill was significantly better than previous down-only models. This is due to its higher loft retention under pressure and its non-compressible nature, which maintains an insulating layer even when compressed.

### Weatherproofing

The 20,000 mm hydrostatic head of the nylon shell effectively repels water from snow and rain. During heavy storms, the bag maintained a dry interior, preventing moisture from condensing inside and reducing the risk of hypothermia.

### Durability Over Time

Durability was assessed through repeated use and exposure to extreme temperatures. The 60D nylon shell showed minimal signs of wear after multiple seasons, maintaining its integrity despite frequent packing and unpacking. The synthetic insulation suffered no significant loss in performance over time, whereas down would have likely started to clump and lose efficiency.

### Comfort and Fit

The sleeping bag's fit was consistent across various body types, making it suitable for a wide range of users. The hooded option provided excellent coverage for the head and neck area, crucial for maintaining warmth during windy nights. The rectangular shape offered ample room without adding unnecessary weight, which is essential in high-altitude environments.
