## Technical Breakdown

### Breathability vs Wind Resistance

Breathability refers to a material's ability to allow air to pass through it, which is crucial in managing body heat and moisture. In contrast, wind resistance measures how well a fabric prevents the passage of cold or hot air, providing insulation against temperature fluctuations. Both factors are essential for comfort during outdoor activities.

### Key Specifications

#### Breathability
- **Hydrostatic Head**: Measured in millimeters (mm), it indicates the height of water a material can resist before leaking through. A higher hydrostatic head suggests better waterproofing.
- **Fabric Denier**: Lower deniers indicate finer, more breathable fabrics. For instance, 20D nylon is typically used for high breathability applications.

#### Wind Resistance
- **R-value**: Measures the thermal resistance of insulation materials and indicates how well they prevent heat transfer. Higher R-values provide better insulation.
- **Fill Power & Down Content**: Describes the quality of down insulation in sleeping bags. Typically, higher fill power (e.g., 600+), with more down content provides superior warmth without bulk.
