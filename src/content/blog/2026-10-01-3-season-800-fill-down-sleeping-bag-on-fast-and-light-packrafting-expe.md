# 3-season 800-fill down sleeping bag on fast-and-light packrafting expeditions: Complete field selection and material breakdown

Fast-and-light (F&L) packrafting involves navigating rivers and waterways with minimal gear, optimizing both weight and portability. ## Key Considerations

### Environmental Conditions
- **Temperature Range**: 3-season bags are designed for temperatures between -10°C (14°F) and +25°C (77°F).
- **Humidity Control**: Damp conditions can significantly reduce insulation effectiveness. A bag that sheds water or has a high hydrostatic head is essential.
- **Wind Resistance**: Wind chill can be a significant factor, especially during the early morning hours.

### Gear Interactions
- **Compatibility with Other Equipment**: The sleeping bag should fit well in your packraft and work seamlessly with other gear like a puffy jacket for layering.
- **Weight and Pack Size**: A lighter sleeping bag reduces overall load but must not compromise on warmth or comfort.

## Material Breakdown

### Outer Shell Fabric
The outer shell is critical for protection against the elements. Common choices include nylon and polyester, both of which are durable yet lightweight:

- **Nylon 2000D (Denier)**: Provides a balance between water resistance and breathability.
- **Polyester 1800D**: Offers better water repellency but is heavier than nylon.

### Insulation Material
Down feathers provide superior warmth-to-weight ratio compared to synthetic insulation:

- **Fill Power of 850+**: High fill power indicates more loft per ounce, offering excellent thermal properties.
- **Hydrophobic Treatment (Down Proof Rating)**: Down should repel moisture; a rating of 3,000mm hydrostatic head is desirable.

### Lining Material
The lining keeps the down from clumping and maintains insulation:

- **Silnylon or Ripstop Fabric**: Lightweight and durable.
- **Microfilament Linings**: Offer better moisture management but are more expensive.

## Detailed Field Analysis

### Insulation Performance
- **Fill Power**: Higher fill power means better loft and more air pockets, which trap heat. A 850+ fill power down bag will maintain its warmth even when compressed.
- **Hydrophobic Treatment**: Down bags should be treated to resist moisture. A hydrostatic head of ≥3,000mm ensures the down stays dry longer, maintaining insulation properties.

### Outer Shell Design
- **Water Resistance**: The outer shell must be robust enough to withstand frequent wet conditions without adding significant weight.
- **Breathability**: Down needs good airflow to maintain its loft and prevent moisture buildup. A balance between water resistance and breathability is critical.

### Lining Material
- **Moisture Management**: A microfilament lining can wick moisture away from the body, keeping you drier at night.
- **Durability**: Silnylon or ripstop fabrics are excellent for durability but might be heavier than other options.

### Packing and Fit
- **Packing Size**: A compact design ensures the bag fits well in a limited space. Most 3-season down bags have packing sizes between 60cm x 75cm.
- **Fit and Comfort**: The sleeping bag should fit snugly but allow for movement. Compression sacks can help reduce volume further.
