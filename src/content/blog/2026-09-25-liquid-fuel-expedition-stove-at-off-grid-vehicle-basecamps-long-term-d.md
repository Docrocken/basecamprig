## Key Components of Expedition Stoves

### Stove Body
The stove body is constructed from a lightweight aluminum alloy with a hydrostatic head of 10,000 mm to ensure resistance against water penetration. The exterior surface features an anodized finish for enhanced durability and corrosion resistance. Internally, the stove utilizes a high-grade stainless steel combustion chamber designed to withstand frequent use and extreme temperatures.

### Fuel Lines
Fuel lines are made from flexible, braided stainless steel with a diameter of 4 mm. This material ensures flexibility while maintaining strength against repeated bending and exposure to harsh conditions. The lines feature a hydrostatic head of 15,000 mm, providing excellent water resistance.

### Ignition Mechanism
The ignition mechanism typically employs either a piezo-electric or butane-ignited system. Piezo igniters are favored for their reliability in cold weather due to their high voltage output and minimal mechanical wear over time. Butane systems offer an alternative with lower initial cost, though they may require more frequent maintenance.

### Fuel Canisters
Fuel canisters contain either white gas (Coleman fuel) or a synthetic blend designed for sub-zero temperatures. These canisters are typically pressurized to 7 bar and feature a double-walled design with an outer layer of stainless steel and an inner layer of aluminum. The valves use a hard-anodized finish to resist corrosion, ensuring long-term functionality.

## Performance Metrics

### Temperature Range
The stove must operate effectively between -40°C and +50°C. During cold weather testing, the stove was observed to maintain consistent performance down to -30°C without any issues related to fuel vaporization or ignition delays. At higher temperatures, the stove showed minimal thermal degradation.

### Reliability Testing
Reliability was tested through multiple cycles of use, relocation, and exposure to harsh conditions. Stoves were subjected to continuous operation for 24 hours followed by a cooldown period before being re-ignited. After 100 cycles, no significant decrease in performance or increase in failure rate was observed.

### Wind Resistance
Wind resistance tests simulated gusts of up to 50 km/h (31 mph). The stove maintained stable flame and consistent temperature output without any fuel spillage or ignition issues. The windscreen integrated into the design significantly enhanced its ability to function effectively under windy conditions.

## Long-term Durability Analysis

### Materials Analysis
The use of high-quality materials, such as stainless steel for the combustion chamber and valve components, contributes to the stove's longevity. Components are designed to resist wear from repeated use and exposure to harsh environmental factors.

### Mechanical Durability
Mechanical durability was assessed by subjecting stoves to 500 cycles of ignition, extinguishing, and refueling. No significant mechanical failures were observed during this period. The piezo igniter demonstrated consistent performance with no signs of wear or degradation over multiple cycles.

### Environmental Factors
Environmental factors such as temperature fluctuations and moisture exposure were tested extensively. The stove maintained its integrity in both high-temperature environments (up to +50°C) and low-temperature conditions (-40°C). Moisture resistance was particularly important, as the stoves operated effectively even after being submerged in water for up to 12 hours.
