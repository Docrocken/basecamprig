Cooking above the tree line on exposed ridges presents unique challenges. These locations are typically characterized by thin air, strong winds, and unpredictable weather conditions, making the selection of cooking equipment crucial for safety and efficiency. This guide focuses on using an insulated titanium [cook pot]([https://www.amazon.com/s?k=](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=)cook+pot+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) in such environments, providing a detailed analysis of essential accessories and modular rig configurations.

### Modular Rig Configuration

#### Basecamp Setup

Setting up the basecamp involves creating a stable cooking platform that can withstand strong winds. Use a combination of rocks, large stones, or an adjustable tripod:

- **Tripod Model**: Adjustable height with anti-slip feet
- **Weight Capacity**: 5 kg (10 lbs)

#### Cold Weather Considerations

When temperatures drop below freezing, consider additional insulating layers for the cook pot and stove. A small heat-insulating mat can be placed under the stove to reduce heat loss:

- **Insulation Mat Dimensions**: 20 x 30 cm
- **Thickness**: 5 mm

#### Wind Protection

Strong winds can significantly impact cooking efficiency, so proper wind protection is crucial:

- **Wind Shield Material**: Flexible silicone with Velcro attachment
- **Size**: Fits most stoves and cook pots

- **Fill Power**: 850+ fill power duck down

A lightweight [backpack]([https://www.amazon.com/s?k=](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=)backpack+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) with a capacity of at least 60 liters for carrying all the gear is recommended. Ensure it has multiple compartments and features such as:
