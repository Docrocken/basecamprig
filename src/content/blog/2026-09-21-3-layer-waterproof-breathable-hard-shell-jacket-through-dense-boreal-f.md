### Repair Guide

#### Identifying Tears
- **Visual Inspection**: Regularly check the seams and areas that are prone to abrasion (underarms, sleeves, lower back) for signs of wear or tears.
- **Light Penetration Test**: Shine a headlamp on the fabric from behind; any light penetration indicates a tear.

#### Tools Required
- **Patch Kit**: Includes adhesive-backed polyurethane patches in various sizes and shapes.
- **Scissors**: For precise cutting of patches.
- **Clean Cloth**: To ensure a clean surface for adhesion.
- **Heat Source**: Such as a camping stove flame or heat gun to activate the adhesive (if applicable).

#### Tear Repair Process
1. **Surface Preparation**
   - Clean the area around and within the tear with a damp cloth to remove dirt, dust, or oils that could affect adhesion.
   - Allow the surface to dry completely before applying any patch.

2. **Application of Patch**
   - Carefully cut the patch to fit snugly over the tear without creating extra slack on the fabric.
   - Apply pressure immediately after placement to ensure proper contact with the jacket’s surface.

3. **Activation and Curing**
   - Activate the adhesive by gently pressing down or using a heat source (if available) for about 30 seconds, ensuring the patch adheres well.
   - Allow the patch to cure at least one hour before resuming normal use. Full curing may take up to 24 hours.

#### Preventive Measures
- **Seam Sealing**: Use seam sealing tape on all seams and areas prone to abrasion during initial setup to prevent small tears from developing into larger ones.
- **Regular Inspection**: Conduct regular visual checks of your jacket’s condition, especially after extended use in abrasive environments or heavy rain.
