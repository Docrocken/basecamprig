## Key Technical Specifications

### Hydrostatic Head Test
The **hydrostatic head** test measures how well a fabric repels water by determining how high a column of water it can resist before it starts to leak. For ultralight bivy sacks intended for use in rocky scree fields and steep mountain passes, the hydrostatic head should be at least 3000 mm, providing excellent protection against light rain or snowfall.

### Abrasion Resistance
Abrasion resistance is critical when navigating rough terrain. Materials used in bivy sacks are often subjected to constant friction from rocks, branches, and other surfaces. A minimum abrasion test rating of 5000 cycles is recommended for durable use in these conditions. This ensures the sack's outer shell remains intact even after repeated exposure.

### Fabric Specifications
- **Outer Shell Material**: Pertex Shield Quantum or similar fabric with a denier between 80 and 120, offering both lightness and durability.
- **Inner Liner Material**: Polyester fleece or synthetic insulation such as PrimaLoft Gold Insulation for added warmth without significantly increasing weight.

### Breathability
Breathable fabrics are essential to prevent condensation buildup inside the bivy sack. The chosen fabric should have a breathability rating of at least 3000 g/m²/24h, allowing moisture to escape while keeping water out.

## Design Considerations

### Ventilation and Airflow
Effective ventilation is crucial for maintaining comfort during long stays in the bivy sack. Strategically placed vents can help regulate temperature by allowing air flow without compromising waterproofing. Ensure that these vents are positioned away from areas prone to abrasion, such as the bottom edge of the sack.

### Weight and Volume
Given the rugged terrain involved, weight and volume are critical factors. An ultralight bivy sack should weigh no more than 200 grams for optimal portability. The compact design must also facilitate easy packing into a backpack or stuff sack without taking up too much space.

## R-value and Insulation

The **R-value** of the insulation directly affects how well the bivy sack retains body heat, especially when exposed to cold mountain passes. A minimum R-value of 3 is recommended for temperatures ranging from -5°C to 0°C, ensuring that users stay warm even in chilly conditions.
