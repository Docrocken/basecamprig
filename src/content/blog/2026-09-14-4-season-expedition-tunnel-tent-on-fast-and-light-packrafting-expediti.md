### Key Material Specifications

#### Membrane Breathability
A membrane's breathability is crucial for maintaining comfortable internal temperatures by allowing moisture vapor to escape while preventing external water intrusion. Common materials used in outdoor [tents](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=tents+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) include Gore-Tex, eVent, and Polartec Neoshell.

- **Gore-Tex Pro**: Offers a hydrostatic head of 20,000 mm and a breathability rating of 30,000 g/m²/day.
- **eVent**: Provides a higher breathability rating at 50,000 g/m²/day with a hydrostatic head of 14,000 mm.
- **Polartec Neoshell**: Features a moderate balance between breathability (28,000 g/m²/day) and hydrostatic head (9,000 mm).

#### Wind Resistance
Wind resistance is vital for minimizing heat loss through convection. This can be achieved by optimizing the tent's structure and materials.

- **Pole Material**: Aluminum poles are preferred for their lightweight nature but may not provide as much rigidity as carbon fiber.
- **Tent Body Fabric**: A combination of nylon or polyester with a DWR (Durable Water Repellent) finish is typical. The Denier rating indicates the fabric's thickness and durability, ranging from 70D to 300D for lightweight applications.

### Field Protocols

#### Setting Up the Tent
Proper setup is essential to maximize the tent's performance. Ensure that [stakes](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=stakes+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) are securely driven into the ground, and guylines are adjusted for optimal tension.

- **Grounding**: Use silicone-treated stakes or metal stakes with a ground anchor system.
- **Guyline Adjustments**: Tension guylines should be equalized to avoid unnecessary strain on the poles and membrane.

#### Maintenance During Expeditions
Regular maintenance ensures that the tent remains functional throughout the expedition.

- **Cleaning**: Remove snow and debris regularly to prevent water retention.
- **Inspection**: Check for any signs of wear or damage, especially around seams and pole connections.
