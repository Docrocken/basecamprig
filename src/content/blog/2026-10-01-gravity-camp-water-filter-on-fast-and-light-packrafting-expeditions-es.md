### 1. Filter Element Selection

- **Ceramic Filters**: Ideal for high-risk environments where multiple contaminants are present. Ceramic filters have a hydrostatic head of up to 500 mm, ensuring efficient water flow while effectively filtering out bacteria, protozoa, and sediment.
- **Activated Carbon Filters**: Best suited for areas with higher levels of organic contaminants or where taste is a concern. Activated carbon filters typically have a lower hydrostatic head (around 300 mm), but excel in removing chlorine, pesticides, and other chemicals.

### 2. Cartridge Holder

The cartridge holder should be durable yet lightweight to ensure minimal impact on overall pack weight. It should accommodate the chosen filter element securely and easily replaceable if damaged or clogged. A robust holder can extend the lifespan of the filter by protecting it from accidental damage during transit.

### 3. Reservoir Bag

- **Material**: The reservoir bag is typically made of food-grade plastic or rubber, ensuring durability without adding significant weight.
- **Capacity**: Depending on the length and intensity of the expedition, a 1 to 2 liter capacity might be sufficient for most scenarios.
- **Drainage System**: A well-designed drainage system helps prevent water from pooling inside the bag, maintaining its structural integrity.
