# Paracord Ridge Line and Tensioners at Remote Winter Basecamp Setups: Field Maintenance Drying and Tear Repair Guide

## Introduction
In winter basecamps where extreme cold temperatures and harsh weather conditions are the norm, the integrity of equipment is crucial for maintaining safety and comfort. This guide focuses on the maintenance, drying, and repair procedures for paracord ridge lines and tensioners used in remote winter setups. Understanding these processes ensures that all essential gear remains functional throughout the season.

## Technical Breakdown
### Material Specifications
- **Paracord:** Typically made from 550 or 750 Denier nylon yarns, with a total diameter of about 1/8 inch (3 mm). The core consists of seven strands of polyester or nylon filaments.
- **Hydrostatic Head:** 400 mm for standard paracord used in most winter setups. This ensures that the cord can withstand light moisture without breaking.
- **Tensile Strength:** Approximately 500 to 700 pounds (2,268 to 3,169 N) per strand.

### Fabric Specifications
- **Bivy Sacks and Tarp:** Often made from a mix of nylon or polyester, with a hydrostatic head of 400 mm. Some models incorporate Gore-Tex for enhanced waterproofing.
- **Sleeping Bags:** Rated to -20°C (-4°F) with a fill power of 650+ and down content ranging from 80% to 90%. The R-value is typically between 1.7 and 2.3, ensuring adequate insulation.

## Field Maintenance
### Drying Procedures
When setting up your basecamp, ensure that all paracord components are thoroughly dried before use. This prevents moisture from weakening the cord over time. Here’s how to dry out paracord:

1. **Hang and Air Dry:** Use a line or hook to hang the [paracord]([https://www.amazon.com/s?k=](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=)[paracord](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=[paracord](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=paracord+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21)+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21)+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) in a well-ventilated area away from direct sunlight.
2. **Heat Drying:** For faster drying, use a hairdryer set on cool or a low heat setting to gently blow air through the cord. Avoid high temperatures that could melt the nylon.
3. **UV Light Exposure:** UV light can help break down moisture molecules, accelerating the drying process without damaging the paracord.

### Cleaning
Regular cleaning is essential to prevent dirt and grime from affecting your paracord’s performance. Use a mild soap solution (1 part water to 10 parts soap) and a soft-bristled brush or cloth. Rinse thoroughly with clean water, then dry as outlined above.

## Repair Procedures
### Identifying Damage
Damage can occur due to wear and tear during use, exposure to harsh conditions, or accidental cuts. Common issues include fraying, breaks, or tears in the paracord.

### Basic Repairs
For minor repairs such as fray prevention or small cuts:

1. **Fray Prevention:** Use a waterproofing spray or a thin layer of paraffin wax applied with a cloth. This creates a barrier against moisture and extends the life of the cord.
2. **Small Cuts:** Apply heat to seal the cut by melting the nylon strands together using a lighter or heat gun at low settings.

### Advanced Repairs
For more extensive damage, replace the section if possible:

1. **Cut Out Damaged Section:** Carefully cut out the damaged portion of the paracord, ensuring that you have enough length to make a secure repair.
2. **Splice Using Paracord Core:** Use the inner yarns from one end of the new paracord to weave through the existing cord at the break point. This creates a strong, seamless connection.

### Tensioner Repair
Tensioners often require more specialized attention:

1. **Check Springs and Mechanisms:** Ensure that all springs are functional and not worn out.
2. **Replace Broken Components:** If components like screws or nuts are damaged, replace them with identical parts from the same manufacturer to maintain consistency in strength and durability.
