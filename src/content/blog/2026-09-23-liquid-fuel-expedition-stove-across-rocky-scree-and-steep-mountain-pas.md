## Technical Breakdown

### Hydrostatic Head Test

The hydrostatic head test measures how well a fabric can resist water penetration when subjected to a column of water. A higher hydrostatic head value indicates better waterproofing capabilities. For this stove setup, we consider a tent with a 10,000 mm hydrostatic head rating. This means the fabric can withstand being submerged in a vertical column of water that reaches 10 meters before it starts to leak through.

### Abrasion Test

## Field Analysis

### Campsite Setup and Conditions

#### Terrain Challenges
Rocky scree fields pose significant challenges for setting up a camp. The uneven surface can make it difficult to secure tent stakes or pegs effectively, potentially leading to water infiltration and wind exposure. Steep mountain passes present additional difficulties due to the incline affecting the stability of the setup.

#### Weather Considerations
Extreme weather conditions are common in these terrains. Cold temperatures, strong winds, and heavy precipitation can significantly impact the performance and durability of equipment. Ensuring that all components have adequate waterproofing and abrasion resistance is critical for maintaining functionality and safety during prolonged use.

### Stove Operation

The alcohol liquid fuel stove selected for this expedition has been chosen due to its high efficiency and compact design. Alcohol stoves typically have a low boiling point, which allows them to ignite quickly even in cold conditions, making them ideal for rapid meal preparation. However, they are less powerful than some other fuels like white gas or kerosene.

#### Setup Procedure
1. **Location Selection**: Choose a flat area away from rocks and debris that could damage the stove.
2. **Stove Placement**: Secure the stove on a stable surface; use heat-resistant pads to protect it from direct contact with hot surfaces.
