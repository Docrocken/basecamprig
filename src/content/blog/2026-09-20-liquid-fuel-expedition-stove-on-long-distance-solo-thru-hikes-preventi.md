## The Mechanism of Condensation

When a liquid fuel stove is used in cold environments (typically below 0°C), water vapor from the combustion process condenses on the cooler surfaces inside the stove or cooking pot, leading to internal moisture. This condensation can be exacerbated by high humidity levels in the air and when using water-rich fuels like white gas.

### Combustion Process

The primary combustion process of a liquid fuel stove involves the evaporation of fuel droplets into vapor, which then combusts with oxygen from the atmosphere. The heat generated during this process is used to cook food or boil water. However, not all the fuel evaporates completely, and some unburned fuel vapors and residual moisture can form condensation on cooler surfaces.

## Preventing Internal Condensation and Moisture Buildup

### Design Considerations for Stoves

To prevent internal condensation, stoves should be designed with materials that minimize moisture absorption and maximize heat retention. Key design elements include:
- **Heat Shields**: These protect the outer body from direct flame exposure, reducing surface cooling.
- **Thermal Insulation**: Materials with high thermal resistance can help retain heat within the stove.

### Material Specifications

#### Outer Body
The outer body of the stove should be made of materials that resist moisture absorption and have good thermal properties. Common choices include:
- **Aluminum**: Lightweight but prone to corrosion in wet environments.
- **Stainless Steel**: Corrosion-resistant, but heavier than aluminum.
- **Titanium**: Lightweight and highly resistant to corrosion.

#### Combustion Chamber
The combustion chamber is a critical component that should be made of materials with high thermal resistance. For instance:
- **Titanium**: Offers excellent heat retention properties while being lightweight.
- **High-Density Aluminum**: Can provide good insulation if properly treated with anodizing or other protective coatings.

### Cooking Pot Selection

Using the right cooking pot can significantly reduce internal condensation:
- **Non-Metallic Coatings**: Pots with non-metallic coatings, such as Teflon or silicone, are less prone to moisture buildup.
- **Thermal Insulation**: Pots made from materials like aluminum or titanium can help retain heat and minimize surface cooling.
