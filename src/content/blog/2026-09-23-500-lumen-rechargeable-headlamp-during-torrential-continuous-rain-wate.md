## Technical Breakdown of Waterproofing

### Hydrostatic Head Test
The hydrostatic head is a measure of the water pressure that can be withstood before penetration occurs. A high hydrostatic head rating indicates superior waterproof performance. For this 500-lumen headlamp, the testing protocol requires it to withstand at least 1200 mm (47 inches) of static water column without leaking.

- **Hydrostatic Head Rating**: 1200 mm
- **Waterproofing Mechanism**: The headlamp uses a combination of seals and gaskets around the lens and charging port, ensuring that water cannot seep inside even during heavy rainfall.

### Abrasion Test
Abrasion resistance is crucial for outdoor gear that will be exposed to rough conditions. This test evaluates how well the outer shell can withstand wear over time without compromising its integrity.

- **Test Method**: The headlamp undergoes abrasion testing using a sandpaper surface with an 80-grit finish, simulating the wear and tear from carrying it in [backpacks]([https://www.amazon.com/s?k=](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=)backpack+outdoor+gear&tag=basecamprig-21) or pockets.
- **Pass Criteria**: The headlamp must pass the test after 150 cycles of abrasion without showing any signs of cracking, peeling, or loss of waterproofing.
